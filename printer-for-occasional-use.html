PRACTICAL TECH GUIDE / 07

Choose a printer for occasional home use

An inexpensive printer can be costly to keep fed with ink. Occasional printing creates a different problem from heavy weekly use: consumables may age before you finish them.

Check before you spend

  1. Estimate how many pages you print each month and whether they are mostly black text, colour documents or photos.
  2. Check replacement cartridge or toner availability and page-yield assumptions, not only the printer price.
  3. Confirm the device supports your computer and phone, plus any scanning or duplex features you genuinely need.

The trade-off

Inkjet is flexible for colour and photos but may need regular use and maintenance. Laser is often simpler for occasional text, although colour models cost more and take more space.

First useful step

Calculate a year of expected consumables from your own page count. If you print only a handful of pages, compare local print-shop costs before buying hardware.
